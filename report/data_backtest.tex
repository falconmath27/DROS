\subsection{Data and backtest methodology}
\label{sec:data}
We construct a long-only exit-timing experiment from daily historical market data.
Each observation is a position entered in one asset on one session; a policy chooses
its exit date, with mandatory liquidation by a fixed deadline. The unit of evaluation
is a hypothetical trade window, rather than a self-financing multi-asset portfolio.

\paragraph{Sources and universe selection.}
Indian and US equities use Yahoo Finance daily closes with split and dividend
adjustment explicitly enabled through \texttt{yfinance}. We freeze the NIFTY~50 list
from the NSE and the S\&P~100 constituent table at retrieval. Binance public spot daily klines supply crypto prices,
with pagination, UTC daily sessions, and an exclusive end date. Crypto selection uses
a frozen retrieval-date market-cap ranking from CoinGecko, excluding identified
stablecoins and wrapped/staked representations. These are current-universe snapshots,
not point-in-time historical constituents; survivorship and selection bias remain.
Original requests are cached with source, retrieval date, symbols, adjustments and
failures. Equity histories cover 2015--2024; crypto is requested from 2017 through 2024.

There are 101 requested US share-class tickers for the 100-company index. SNDK has no
usable history in the frozen interval. Of ten requested crypto assets, FIGR\_HELOC
has no requested Binance spot pair and HYPE has no observations in the interval;
eight are retained, including BTC and ETH. These exclusions are recorded, rather
than replaced by simulated prices. Later listings contribute only after their own
feature lookback is available. Missing terminal delisting proceeds are not imputed.
FX and gold are omitted under the project's reduced-scope fallback; CSV/custom-symbol
loaders support subsequent extensions with appropriate cost and currency assumptions.

\paragraph{Cleaning and calendars.}
We sort and deduplicate session dates, convert nonpositive/nonfinite prices to missing,
exclude stablecoins, and log coverage and exclusions. Missing observations remain
missing by default: there is no backward filling, interpolation, or extrapolation
before listing or after the last observed price. Windows containing missing prices
or features are discarded. Extreme log returns are flagged against preceding rolling
statistics and retained, so a genuine crash is not removed as a data error. Equity
sessions use observed dates within each local panel; crypto is reindexed to every UTC
day. This does not reconstruct exchange-specific historical holidays or delisting cashflows.

\paragraph{Windows and observable features.}
The default deadline is 30 calendar days after entry, with exit on the last observed
session on or before that deadline. Entries are spaced five panel observations apart.
Windows without a complete horizon are excluded. Because calendar-month windows
contain different numbers of sessions, arrays repeat the terminal state, reward and
date up to 31 states; stopping indices are clipped to each window's true terminal
index. Padding creates no additional price movement or holding opportunity.

Let $S_0$ be the entry reference price and $S_j$ the current price. Features include
$S_j/S_0-1$, running drawdown $S_j/\max_{k\leq j}S_k-1$, remaining time, the current
daily log return, 20-observation trailing volatility, and log returns lagged by one,
two and five observations. Volatility uses annualization factors 252 for equities
and 365 for crypto. Local index and volatility-index levels (NIFTY/India VIX or
S\&P~500/VIX) are shifted one source observation before backward date alignment.
This prevents using an external close that becomes available later than the decision.
Feature construction is causal; any fitted scaling is learned on training data only.

\paragraph{Execution convention and costs.}
We use a stylized daily-close reference-price model. The current close and current
exit reward are observable inputs, consistent with the existing stopping-method
interface. We do not claim that an order submitted after observing a close can fill
at that close. Next-open execution would require separating unobserved execution
payoffs from policy inputs, which is outside this close-mark experiment.

Writing $c_b,c_s$ for proportional entry/exit friction, $f$ for a simple daily funding
assumption and $d_j$ for elapsed calendar days, the undiscounted net return is
\[
 r_j=\frac{S_j}{S_0}\frac{1-c_s}{1+c_b}-1-fd_j.
\]
Both legs include fees, half-spread and slippage. Research fee scenarios per side are
12 basis points for NSE, one for US equities and ten for spot crypto; assumed
half-spread/slippage are respectively 2/2, 1/1 and 2/2 basis points. These are explicit
sensitivity assumptions, not official historical tariffs. Spot funding and discounting
default to zero. A configurable perpetual-funding scenario represents an initial-notional
charge, not a leveraged margin-account backtest. Cost multipliers and JSON overrides
support sensitivity studies. The default reward is $R_j=r_j$; a second available
objective is exponential utility $(1-e^{-a r_j})/a$, with raw net returns saved separately.

\paragraph{Chronological evaluation and rules.}
Expanding walk-forward folds start with 36 months of training, six months validation
and six months test, advancing by six months. All assets share date boundaries.
A training or validation window is purged if its scheduled outcome horizon crosses
the next boundary; an optional extra embargo is configurable. Test periods are disjoint.
Trailing test features may use genuinely available earlier history. No threshold or
model choice uses test outcomes. Fixed comparison rules are hold-to-deadline,
take-profit/stop-loss at $+5\%/-3\%$, and a $5\%$ trailing drawdown exit. Rules observe
daily closes, rather than claiming intraday stop fills.

\paragraph{Validation and statistical handoff.}
The harness exports aligned asset IDs, unique window IDs, entry dates, all decision
dates, deadlines, exit dates, realized rewards and raw net returns. All methods within
a fold use identical test windows. Offline checks verify reward accounting, causal
features and rules, boundary purging, missing-data behavior, calendar padding, cache
replay, pagination, and metadata round trips. A real-data smoke run exercises every
existing learned-method family with deliberately shortened neural training; it is
an integration check, not the final learned-model comparison. Existing methods retain
their global date-zero rule, originally designed for common simulated initial states;
heterogeneous market entry states are a limitation. Overlapping windows and correlated
assets invalidate independent-window standard errors. P5 receives dated per-path
outputs for block-bootstrap inference, risk-sensitive comparisons and regime analysis.
Mean trade returns are not interpreted as portfolio returns or annualized performance.
